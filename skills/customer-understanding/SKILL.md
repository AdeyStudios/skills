# Customer understanding

Working draft. Guidance has not yet demonstrated improved business outcomes.

Explain whose problem is present, what progress they seek, what they currently do and what evidence would change the product or message decision.

## Research

Read available project positioning and the actual evidence before requesting more input. Identify how each source was collected, the questions participants saw, its date and the decision it can support. Keep the user, payer and purchaser distinct where relevant.

Segment by consequential differences: use case, acquisition source, new/returning, paying/trial/free access and maturity of use. Do not let staff, test accounts or a heavily prompted survey dominate conclusions about buyers. Consent-filtered telemetry describes the measured cohort; it is not automatically the whole customer base.

## Execute

- Extract concrete situations, desired functional/emotional/social outcomes, previous alternatives, workarounds, triggers, barriers, reasons for choosing or leaving, and phrases worth preserving. Use short faithful excerpts with evidence identifiers; do not fabricate quotations or composite people.
- Connect statements to observed behaviour only when the records and permissions support that join. Saying a feature matters, using it and paying because of it are different observations. A replay can reveal a failure without establishing the user's motive.
- Keep a small evidence map: finding, relevant segment, supporting records, counterexamples, confidence and implication. Show counts and denominators when justified; label non-response and overlapping themes rather than summing them into fictional totals.
- Distinguish a bug, confusing interaction, missing capability, weak repeated value and a mismatch of expectations. Offer alternative explanations before prescribing more marketing.
- Translate findings into a customer-language brief and a small number of product, positioning or message hypotheses. Tie each proposal to a result the customer wants and credible product support. Keep persona-like summaries provisional and avoid invented demographic detail.
- If evidence cannot answer the decision, propose a focused observation, interview or survey question. When available, customer-discovery can help design that work, but this skill remains usable independently.

## Verify

Check the leading conclusions against the original evidence and contradictory segments. Report what customers said separately from interpretation and causal claims. Explain which uncertainty materially blocks a decision and what would resolve it. Avoid claiming product-market fit from enthusiasm or a small sample.

Use only authorised records and keep identities and raw evidence in the project's permitted storage. Research or analysis does not authorise outreach. A copywriting handoff should carry the audience, outcome, exact language and limitations, not just a slogan.
