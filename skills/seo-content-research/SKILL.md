# SEO content research

Find learning needs the product can answer well enough to deserve a visit. Choose content by evidence and usefulness, not equal coverage of product categories or an arbitrary publishing quota.

This is a working draft. Its recommendations need verification against real pages and subsequent outcomes, not acceptance because the report is detailed.

## Establish context

Use the supplied product, audience, existing pages, commercial goal and constraints. Inspect available project documentation and existing content before asking for information already present. Separate the research question from an assumed answer: an existing topic list is not evidence of search demand.

Use authorised first-party search and conversion data when available. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the work; continue research that does not depend on it.

Inspect prior research, published pages and work already in progress. Record source dates and the current deployment. Do not turn historical findings into a duplicate backlog. Keep a site-wide technical audit separate from the task of deciding which content deserves work.

## Investigate opportunities

- Group related searches by the underlying need and likely intent. Avoid a separate page for every keyword variant.
- Review current search results for the intended market and language. Record representative queries, sources and research date. Results are a sample that can vary by location and personalisation.
- Inspect the actual leading answers: accuracy, audience, clarity, examples, completeness and format. Explain the specific contribution our page could make. Do not assume established results are poor or easy to outrank.
- Use Search Console, keyword tools or other available evidence to assess demand. Search results, related questions and suggestions demonstrate wording or intent, not verified monthly volume. Do not invent volumes, difficulty scores or traffic forecasts. If demand is unmeasured, label it unmeasured.
- Check whether existing pages should be improved or consolidated before proposing new ones. Keep one page per distinct need, with related questions covered together.
- Connect each candidate to a plausible next useful product action without making every educational page a sales pitch.

### Turn search observations into candidates

For each cluster, record the actual queries, existing matching URLs and evidence window. Search Console impressions describe this property's exposure, not total market search volume. Separate branded, category/product, informational and comparison needs where that changes the opportunity.

Use query-by-page data to establish which URL receives a query; separate query and page totals cannot establish that match. Check country, device, page age and position before judging CTR. A page near positions 8 to 30 can be worth investigating, but a few impressions do not prove demand, a content gap or an easy win. Multiple ranking URLs do not by themselves prove harmful cannibalisation.

Inspect the live answer and current leading results before prescribing more examples, longer copy or a new article. If a matched page already satisfies the need, retain it unless another verified obstacle is worth addressing. Group overlapping keyword variants into one need and document the exact members when summing cluster evidence.

## Connect audience evidence to search demand

Public posts, reviews and customer wording can supply candidate needs and useful phrasing. Keep the source, audience, market and date; repeated posts or high engagement do not establish search volume or buying intent. Validate candidates against current search results and existing pages before commissioning content.

When quantitative tools are available, expand a small seed set, deduplicate and batch compatible keyword lookups where the live API supports it. Sample provider coverage and prices before scaling. Record location, language, collection date and whether a value is a provider estimate, range or observed property metric. CPC and advertising competition describe the ad market; neither is organic ranking difficulty. Avoid allocating an SEO or ads budget from volume share alone.

Use seasonality and query intent to interpret apparent opportunities. Compare terms from compatible markets and windows; related variants can overlap. A high-volume term can still be a poor product fit. See [demand research](references/demand-research.md) for the evidence distinctions and retrieval decisions.

## Make the recommendation concrete

For the strongest opportunities, provide the proposed title and page type, audience and intent, evidence, current answer gap, our useful contribution, product fit and uncertainty. Explain the ordering. A smaller defensible shortlist is better than padding weak recommendations.

Make the choice explicit: improve, consolidate, create, investigate, wait or reject. Compare likely relevant audience, achievable contribution, product fit, confidence, effort and dependencies. Prefer a verified useful improvement to an existing page over an unsupported new-page idea; a requested number is a target, not a reason to invent opportunities. Do not assign precise ROI, difficulty or traffic estimates without supporting data.

For an educational article, outline a clear explanation, concrete worked example, important misconception and optional fresh application scenario. Give value before asking the reader to act. Visual subjects should use meaningful original or appropriately licensed illustrations when they improve understanding. Do not add a test or a comparison section merely to fill a template.

Keep proposed titles distinct from verified keywords. Cite primary sources for technical or factual claims, and source search evidence separately from the article's teaching material. If browsing or analytics is unavailable, produce a provisional hypothesis list and describe what needs checking.

## Measure and learn

Specify what would justify revisiting the recommendation: indexing, relevant impressions and clicks, useful engagement and downstream activation or conversion. Match measurements to the actual product; avoid promising rankings or interpreting new pages' short observation windows as failure.

Record the baseline window, URL/query cohort, actual change date, proposed mechanism and next review condition. Compare equivalent final-data windows with consistent filters, noting seasonality, releases and audience mix. Recheck implementation before evaluating traffic. With sparse data, extend observation or report uncertainty rather than repeatedly changing titles. If exposure grows without useful visits, investigate relevance and search context; if visits grow without activation, inspect the product transition when authorised conversion data exists. Neither pattern proves its cause alone.

Carry prior decisions forward in recurring work. Reopen them only for new evidence or a changed premise. Preserve an honest no-change decision when the likely return is low. A research result, a merged PR and measured customer growth are different outcomes.

Research does not itself authorise publishing pages, changing analytics or creating accounts. Implement content only within the user's requested scope.
