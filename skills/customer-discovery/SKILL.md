# Customer discovery

> Early working draft. Add sourced examples, failure cases and behavioural evaluations before relying on this as a finished method.

Discover the customer's actual problem and choices, rather than collecting compliments about a solution.

## Research

- Define the learning question, target customer and decision the research should change. Specify who is using, buying or paying when those roles differ.
- Inspect existing evidence where authorised: actual behaviour, support messages, interviews, reviews, relevant competitors and survey responses. Separate direct observations from interpretations.
- Check selection bias, small samples, internal/test accounts and entitlements. Someone receiving access for free may still offer useful UX feedback, but their stated willingness to pay cannot be interpreted like that of a prospective payer.

## Execute

- Choose the lightest appropriate discovery method: a few genuine conversations, usability sessions, observed tasks, short surveys, support/review synthesis or a targeted recruitment plan. Talking to users is one method, **not** a requirement to cold-call or interview everyone.
- Prefer questions about past behaviour, concrete alternatives, frequency, cost, decision triggers and attempted workarounds. Avoid leading users toward a preferred answer.
- When gathering responses, seek permission and respect privacy. Do not fabricate interviews, quotes, personas or outreach; do not contact customers without explicit authorisation.
- Summarise the distinct patterns, strongest counterexamples and uncertainty. Recommend a single product or business hypothesis to investigate next.

## Verify

- Check who actually responded and what question they saw, including dates, segments, selection method, missing answers and competing interpretations.
- Make findings traceable to authorised evidence without exposing personal customer information in public artifacts.
- State what would confirm or overturn the leading interpretation and what decision the founder can responsibly make now. Do not call a handful of opinions product-market fit.
