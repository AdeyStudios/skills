# Video editing

Working draft. Make a video worth watching at its intended speed. Judge it against relevant human-edited work and the user's brief, without lowering the standard because the edit was automated. A functioning renderer, attractive contact sheet or clever effect is not evidence of an engaging edit.

## Research

### Establish the actual job

- Recover the audience, purpose, platform, length, format, audio plan and requested depth from the conversation and project. A runway editorial, product demonstration and outfit-change reel need different hooks. Keep a narrow revision narrow.
- Establish the viewing surfaces before laying out text: full-screen playback, feed preview and profile/cover can crop differently, while usernames, captions and controls cover other areas. For TikTok or Reels, read [placement and safe areas](references/placement-safe-areas.md) before composition; a clean 9:16 canvas is not the viewer's unobstructed screen.
- When taste or priorities remain unclear, ask one or two consequential questions: a reference the user would publish, what should make the viewer stay, or whether breadth of looks or a few stronger moments matters more. Continue inspecting assets while awaiting optional answers. Do not require the user to design the edit.
- Read [fashion editing](references/fashion-editing.md) for runway, apparel and accessory work. Read [research and its limits](references/research.md) when researching platform guidance, trends or reference edits; do not turn advertising recommendations into universal rules for organic posts.

### Obtain and understand the material

- **Supplied files or link:** use those assets. Resolve a page link to its actual media through available authorised tools. Preserve the original and source URL. Do not silently substitute another show, collection or creator.
- **Search brief:** translate the brief into required images and actions before searching. Verify the collection/event, publisher, date and actual accessible footage. Prefer relevant original sources; preserve attribution and note reuse restrictions. Public availability does not establish publication rights.
- **Insufficient access:** state what cannot be obtained or inspected, then request the smallest missing input. Do not bypass protected access, invent source contents, or claim to have watched an inaccessible video.
- Inspect the actual motion and source edits, alongside an overview of frames. Log usable intervals, strong movement, details, existing cuts, resolution, frame rate, orientation and audio. Contact sheets help selection but cannot establish continuity between samples.
- The optional [inspection helper](scripts/inspect_video.py) produces timed contact sheets, candidate cuts and exact frame provenance. See [tools and finishing](references/tools.md). Re-extract production images at full resolution; overview thumbnails are not final assets.

## Execute

### Find the editorial idea and the hook

- Define what the viewer gets: a striking transformation, a comparison, an unexpected relationship, a detail revealed, a satisfying movement or another specific payoff. Name the premise independently of the transition or software used to create it.
- For a brief asking for a distinctive concept, test whether that idea is visible in the sequence. Naming a motif such as “movement” does not make a shortened runway compilation conceptually original. A selection-only assembly can be a useful rough cut; develop the requested idea through relationships, reveals, framing, structure or another justified intervention before calling it complete. Additional effects alone do not satisfy this check.
- Consider materially different opening approaches internally. Compare what the viewer actually sees first and what makes the next moment worth watching. When variants are requested, vary the premise, selection or attention arc rather than placing different effects over the same assembly.
- For several edits from one source, compare the variants before rendering: what does each reveal, which material makes that possible, and how does its sequence develop? Let the footage suggest the idea. Read [production decisions](references/production.md) for batch planning, live graphic treatments and subject tracking.
- Put compelling visual information in the first frame and advance it promptly. A short edit cannot spend a large share of its runtime preparing to become interesting. A brand or title can be the hook when its transformation earns attention; do not default to a static logo lead-in.
- Establish a real reason to continue and deliver it. Avoid generic “wait for it” text, unrelated surprise footage, and a spectacular opening that the rest of the edit cannot support.
- Check that the connection reads in the images before explaining or naming it. An abstract concept label cannot rescue an unclear sequence; simplify the relationship or remove the label. Identity and factual context can still be useful text.
- Build a timing sketch or short hook proof using real assets before polishing the whole piece. Review the early sequence at normal speed. If it feels slow, change selection, order, event timing or duration before adding effects or colour polish.

### Shape the rhythm

- Build phrases with contrast: a quick accent, a readable reveal, an acceleration or a held moment that earns its time. Equal-length shots, constant slow motion and identical easing at every change usually flatten the rhythm.
- Increase meaningful visual information, not merely cut count or camera speed. Remove repeated poses, approach time, decorative travel and polite pauses that add no discovery. Use short details around clear outfit or product anchors when that suits the brief.
- Check dense image runs separately: can the viewer register each image's intended subject at delivery speed? Slow the run, reduce the number of images or establish a readable anchor when it becomes an indistinguishable burst. Keep momentum through moving footage and well-timed handoffs rather than inserting freeze pauses to make unreadable cuts longer.
- Let the source action determine in/out points. Cut on an expressive gesture, a change of direction, a fabric peak, a reveal or a useful compositional match. A whole outfit needs a different reading time from a shoe, texture or familiar repeated motif.
- Carry direction and energy through a join when continuity is intended. Check position and velocity at the handoff: easing can hide neither a starting jump nor a repeated stop-and-restart pattern. A motivated hard cut may flow better than a long dissolve.
- Use ramps selectively around events. Accelerate low-information travel and preserve the expressive instant. Do not stretch every source shot to the same duration, reverse walking to fill time, or trust interpolation around hands, legs, hems and overlapping bodies.
- Match an actual supplied track when requested, considering phrases and accents rather than cutting on every beat. If music will be added later, make the visual rhythm work independently and label delivery silent. Do not invent a soundtrack or claim beat matching without analysing the audio.

### Make the treatment serve the piece

- Use framing, typography, stills, composites and transitions to focus attention or connect ideas. Stills and live action can play different roles in a phrase; do not animate every image by default. Research images may become creative material, but there is no obligation to use everything extracted. Do not turn one praised example into a reusable formula.
- Keep essential headlines, subtitles, logos, URLs and the visual payoff inside the usable area shared by the intended placements. Do not default to titles at the top edge or branding at the bottom. Check the entire text block and its animated reading interval against both crop and UI masks; backgrounds and decorative material can still bleed. Recompose or make placement-specific versions when needed rather than shrinking everything into an unreadable centre panel.
- Keep the subject convincing. Rough masks, false angles, warped garments, distracting ghost figures and blurry enlargements are quality failures even when the technique is technically impressive. A crop is not a newly filmed camera angle.
- Treat full-look, detail and motion views as choices with a purpose. Do not keep navigation through a graphic layout longer than the visual discovery it enables.
- Grade and finish after the timing works. Preserve intentional garment colours, skin and readable fabric detail; protect highlights and shadows. Do not use a global colour cast as a substitute for a clear colour-led concept.
- Choose the simplest reliable toolchain that can achieve the idea. Respect requested applications and identify which tool actually performed each stage. A preferred tool's access problem should not consume the whole session while the edit remains unproven. See [tools and finishing](references/tools.md) for adapters and recovery.

## Verify

### Review the edit, then the file

- Inspect playback at delivery speed and phone viewing size whenever available. Watch the first frame, first second, hook payoff, rhythm changes and ending. Slow or frame-step only to diagnose a problem; an effect that works only paused has not passed a motion review.
- Ask whether the idea reads without an explanation, each change adds something, the strongest material arrives early, the clothes/product register, and the energy develops rather than mechanically repeating. Trim another pass if a moment can disappear without losing meaning or pleasure.
- Compare the rough cut with a relevant reference on opening speed, visual information, readable anchors, rhythmic contrast and payoff. Use a fair comparison; different footage and audio can explain differences. Pretty frames and compliments do not establish competitive editing or audience retention.
- Check accidental source cuts, blank frames, crops, mask edges, speed changes, interpolation, type and platform UI obstructions. Preview the actual export, not just the composition before encoding. If playback is unavailable, use dense temporal sequences around joins and explicitly disclose the review limit.
- Inspect the encoded hook, key text states, payoff and cover at phone size with the intended crop and interface overlays visible. Check caption expansion, the button rail and end branding. Use current placement templates or actual preview evidence; the optional [placement preview helper](scripts/preview_placement.py) provides labelled fallback masks and crop stress tests, not a guarantee of current app behaviour. Report simulated checks separately from an actual in-app preview.
- Verify decoding, duration, dimensions, frame rate, colour/range metadata and requested audio state. Technical checks establish file integrity, not taste or impact. Preserve originals and earlier versions unless the user asks to remove them.
- Deliver the export and editable project/script with source provenance. Report the actual tools, meaningful changes and review limitations briefly. An edit request does not itself authorise publishing, sending messages, buying assets or changing campaign settings.
- Keep deliverables easy to find: separate final videos from thumbnails, drafts and source footage. When files are already saved locally, show their location rather than downloading or archiving them again unless requested. A batch viewing page is optional; each video should also be directly accessible.

### Learn from the result

- Use specific feedback to revise the weakest part: first-second interest, pace, readability, continuity, concept or finish. Re-test the changed sequence instead of generating an entire batch of speculative alternatives.
- Keep project-specific taste and raw analytics with that project. Improve this generic skill only when a lesson changes a reusable decision; avoid accumulating an effects catalogue or ranking a user's examples as universal templates.
- When audience data is available, compare early retention, watch time, completion and rewatch behaviour in context. Distinguish creative acceptance, a technically tested export and observed performance. Without audience data, do not claim the edit will go viral or outperform a human editor.
