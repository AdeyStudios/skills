# Fashion editing: attention with readable clothes

Use this reference for runway, styling, apparel and accessory edits. It is an editorial framework to test, not a template or a requirement to use a particular effect.

## Choose what the viewer is discovering

An outfit can be the subject, a detail can organise the piece, or a change can provide the payoff. Decide which applies before choosing transitions.

| Possible premise | What earns the next moment | What can fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detail becomes silhouette | A compelling texture, fastening or movement resolves into the look | A soft, over-enlarged detail delays the recognisable garment |
| One feature across several looks | The viewer spots a colour, shape or accessory recurring in different contexts | A title announces a connection the selected footage does not demonstrate |
| Styling or outfit transformation | A gesture completes as the look changes | Mismatched body position, an obscured outfit or an unearned reveal |
| Comparison | A meaningful contrast changes how the viewer sees the clothes | A parade of unrelated items with no comparison to make |
| Graphic idea becomes live footage | The graphic changes how the image is encountered, then gives way to the subject | A long title, repeated layout navigation or a trick that eclipses the clothes |
| Movement or attitude | A distinctive stride, turn, fabric action or expression develops | An almost unchanged walk held because it is attractive footage |

These illustrate reasons to watch, not a fixed menu. Invent a more suitable idea when the material supports one. A concept is stronger than a technique name such as “zoom edit” or “velocity.”

## Make a useful shot log

For each candidate interval record: source in/out, existing cuts, look/detail, movement phase, silhouette readability and a reason to use it. Find strong moments inside the shot rather than always beginning at its first frame.

Inspect a few frames before and after intended boundaries. A source video may already cut between models. Select an interval entirely within one source shot when the planned treatment relies on continuity.

For a still-to-motion handoff, use the actual source frame and matching crop. Record its decoded frame index or timestamp; do not approximate the pose from a contact sheet. If returning to a still, update it to the frame where movement stops. This is a technical continuity requirement when using that treatment, not a recommendation to repeat the treatment.

## Build speed with contrast

Start with the best available action, composition or unresolved visual event. Avoid requiring viewers to wait through establishing footage or read a neutral title before anything happens.

Use quick accents to direct attention, then give a silhouette, comparison or payoff enough time to read. Test duration against the actual image: an unfamiliar layered look can need longer than a shoe flash. Do not apply one minimum shot length to every image or turn “fast” into an uninterrupted chain of unreadable fragments.

An image run can feel energetic yet fail because no look registers. Review the run as its own phrase at normal speed; if its purpose is to show three looks, viewers must be able to recognise three looks. Lengthen those glimpses or choose fewer, stronger images without freezing the preceding walk. Very short inserts may still serve as texture or an accent, but do not count that as readable outfit coverage.

Map new visual information on the rough timeline. A different pose, detail, scale, location or resolved expectation can advance the piece. Movement alone is insufficient: a slow camera orbit around an image the viewer already understands may add seconds without adding interest.

Check the first frame, 0.5 seconds, 1 second and 2 seconds as diagnostic views. These are review checkpoints, not universal algorithm thresholds. Ask what is already compelling and what has changed. If only the software's animation has progressed, reconsider the opening.

Review neighbouring joins as a phrase. Two individually smooth moves can still feel mechanical if both arrive, settle, pause and repeat. Preserve momentum or make a decisive cut; do not hide every editorial choice in a soft wipe.

Vary the kind of image when it helps the idea: live action can establish an event, a closer view can explain it, and a held editorial image can punctuate the phrase. A still need not acquire an artificial zoom to belong in a moving edit. Judge its entrance, reading time and relationship to neighbouring motion. Distinguish a purposeful change of visual texture from a frozen frame caused by a rendering failure.

For event-led footage, preserve enough setup to understand the action, land the event, then decide whether a closer view or a still adds a payoff. Do not trim away the very anticipation that makes the action meaningful. Atmospheric footage can earn an opening through composition, changing light or emerging action; a longer opening is not automatically dead time, just as short shots are not automatically engaging.

## Use sound deliberately

With a supplied track, locate its phrases, changes and a few useful accents. Decide where the edit builds or releases relative to those events. A clip need not change at every beat. Preserve image readability and avoid assuming all peaks deserve the same visual hit.

For silent delivery, make the picture's timing independently purposeful. It can anticipate later music without pretending to fit an unknown track. A naturally occurring footstep, fabric sound or room tone can matter when audio is wanted; repeated generic whooshes are not an automatic improvement.

## Review against a real reference

Choose references that match the task's audience and purpose. Luxury editorial, streetwear montage, a try-on and a shop demonstration can all be effective for different reasons. Do not import a seller's CTA, a creator's persona or a lo-fi appearance merely because the reference is on TikTok.

For each accessible reference note:

- What appears in the first frame and what happens next?
- Where does the premise become clear, and where is its payoff?
- Which changes are cuts, movement inside a shot, retiming, graphics or audio?
- Where does the edit accelerate, hold or release, and why?
- What lets the clothes remain understandable?
- What can the supplied footage support without inventing angles or details?

Record whether this came from playback, timed frames, a transcript or a publisher's account. Public views and likes do not establish that one transition caused engagement. Read [the source notes](research.md) for the current evidence and its limits.

Check whether apparent cut candidates are actually changes in lighting or exposure. Strobes and bright-to-dark passages can trigger scene detection within one continuous shot. Use nearby frames to distinguish the edit from the original event before deriving cut frequency or transition rules.
